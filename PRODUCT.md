# Bjärke — product context

Compiled 2026-11-16 from the team's own documents (persona questionnaire, validation funnel
worksheet, messaging & direction summary, market validation prep, guerilla user test, IYA funding
application) and the Figma file. Use this as the source of truth for copy and claims.
Where the documents disagree, the disagreement is listed under "Open decisions" — do not pick a side silently.

## What it is
A personal safety pendant worn like a necklace, for solo moments: the ride home, the walk to the door,
a bar where you get separated from friends, a first date. Team: Oliver Pederson, Ansh Sankhala, Zain Sy,
Kai Dal Bello (USC Iovine and Young Academy, ACAD-185). Stage: concept and validation; a working
prototype is being built. Features below are design targets, not a shipped product.

Name: from Swedish "Björn" (bear); the team reads Bjärke as "little bear". A small bear motif is an option.

## Core features (design targets)
1. Double press the pendant to alert a trusted contact and share live location. No phone unlock needed.
2. Press to record a short clip, with a visible recording light. Footage stays encrypted on the pendant
   unless the wearer shares it.
3. Swappable shells so it reads as an accessory: signal red with off-white, plus yellow, blue, purple, teal.
   A premium gold model is also planned.
4. Companion app: manage trusted contacts, friend check-ins, linked pendants ("everyone's home").
5. Battery lasts a full night out; water resistant.
6. Optional 911 connection (listed in two docs; unconfirmed).

## Value propositions, in priority order
1. Help without unlocking your phone. Feeling: calm, never alone, like bringing a trusted friend along.
2. Evidence on your terms. Feeling: in control, not creepy.
3. Something you actually want to wear. "A safety device in a drawer protects nobody."

## Non-negotiables (from the messaging doc)
- Recording is always visible and only happens when the wearer asks for it. Nothing always-on, nothing hidden.
- The wearer owns her footage and location. Nothing is shared unless she chooses.
- The alert has to work every time.
- It always has to look good.

## Who it is for
Primary: women 18–30 in cities or on campus who go out and sometimes get home alone.
- "Jen", 23, NYC, recent Brown graduate in investment banking. Goes to bars and clubs, takes rideshares home alone.
  Uses TikTok, Instagram, Reddit, Google, Amazon reviews. Much of her profile is marked as hypothesis to validate.
- "Samantha", 22, senior at TCU. Texts a friend or shares location before a ride, then puts her phone away.
Secondary audiences: parents buying for a daughter going to college, late-shift workers, runners,
women meeting first dates, rideshare drivers.

Dream outcome (Jen): leave a bar or rideshare, reach a trusted person quickly if needed, and confirm she got
home, without giving up privacy or wearing a conspicuous camera.

## Objections the page must answer
- Why wear another device when my phone or watch already has safety features?
- Does it need my phone nearby? Does it work with a locked or dead phone, or with no signal?
- Who gets the alert, how do I know it went through, and can I cancel an accidental press?
- What is recorded and stored, and who can see it? Is a camera necklace creepy to people around me?
- Is there a subscription? What is the full cost?
- Battery life, and can it be recharged?
- Is this a false sense of security?

## Tone
Calm, direct, transparent. Optimistic and empowering, never fear-based. Friendly and a little playful.
Describe "a way to reach someone you trust", not "protection". Never imply the device guarantees safety.
Show the alert flow and state the limits honestly.
She prefers short videos showing exactly how it works, real-person reviews and honest FAQs.

## Brand and visual direction (from the docs)
Bright and friendly: signal red with off-white, colored shells. Motifs: rounded pill shape, single lens,
one button. Mood: flash photos from a night out, a city street at 1am, the back seat of an Uber,
matte silicone, film-camera grain. References: Teenage Engineering, IKEA, Nothing, Gentle Monster.

## Offer and funnel
- Traffic: Facebook/Instagram ads. The course notes 90% of landing-page traffic is mobile.
- Ad headline: "Staying Safe Doesnt Mean You Cant Be Chic".
- Funnel: ad → landing page (hero, sign-up form, three benefit sections) → reservation page.
- CTA: "Reserve My Launch Invite". Sign-up text: "We're Launching Soon — get an exclusive discount by signing up now".
- Pricing (validation funnel worksheet):
  - Color model: $1 deposit, $70 at ship, MSRP $125 (44% off).
  - Gold model: $1 deposit, $130 at ship, MSRP $200 (35% off).
- Reservation form fields in use (Google Sheet via Apps Script): name, email, phone (optional), shell,
  getting home, batch option.
- Traction goal: 300+ waitlist sign-ups.

## Funnel copy already written by the team
- Hero: "The days of staying on call with your friend while in an odd uber are over."
- "One press tells your people exactly where you are" — pair and pick contacts; double press when
  something feels off; your friend gets your live location.
- "Proof when you need it, privacy when you don't" — records only when you press, with a light.
- "Swap the shell to match whatever you're wearing tonight".
- Headlines to test: "Help is one press away." / "Wear it because it is cute, press it because it matters." /
  "Nobody has to know you just asked for help."

## Competitors and where they fall short
- invisaWear: jewelry look, ADT-backed, but needs the phone in Bluetooth range, app must stay open,
  subscription complaints, battery cannot be recharged.
- Flare: bracelet with a fake phone call; iPhone only, needs phone in range, useless without membership.
- She's Birdie: loud keychain alarm; simple and affordable, not discreet.
- Cerret: Swiss fine-jewelry bracelet, no subscription, replaceable battery; expensive.
- Already on her phone: Apple Check In, Apple Watch SOS, Noonlight, Life360.
Bjärke's intended edge: wearable people want to wear, evidence under the wearer's control, and (if real)
its own connection so it works when the phone is dead or out of reach.

## What testers said (guerilla user test, 3 testers)
- Understood the product and the reserve action (mostly 4–5 out of 5).
- "Would you convert?" scored 3, 2, 3 out of 5.
- Advice: too much text and the writing sounds generic; looks too AI; the product image is not a proper rendering.

## Open decisions (resolve before writing final copy)
1. Recording: the messaging doc says press-only and "never records on its own". The current site
   (index.html, device.html) and the user-test sheet describe a camera that watches all night and records
   automatically. The funding application calls automatic detection a later exploration. These conflict.
2. Does the pendant have its own connection, or alert through the phone over Bluetooth? Docs say both.
3. Is the light a recording indicator, or a deterrent flash? The site says deterrent; the docs say indicator.
4. Visual direction: the docs call for bright, playful, signal red. The current site is minimal black and white.
5. Pricing shown on the form ("Standard release, $125") does not match the $70 launch price.
6. Shell line-up and whether the gold model is offered at launch. Current hero photos show gold, silver
   and a black clover pendant, none of which match the colored pill shells in the docs.
7. Subscription or one-time purchase. Not decided.
8. Product imagery: testers want a real render, not AI-generated lifestyle images.
